# Amescom website planning and Cursor handoff

Prepared 3 November 2026. This pack defines a proposed public company website using the five supplied documents. It includes questions for the business owner and a sequence of implementation prompts. It does not treat product specifications as instructions to build those products into the website.

## Start here

1. Read `01-Requirements.md` for the proposed scope and the evidence behind it.
2. Answer the first ten questions in `02-Questions.md`. The remaining questions can be assigned to product, marketing, technical and operations owners.
3. Record answers in `06-Decisions.md`. Unanswered items remain assumptions or launch dependencies.
4. Give Cursor this entire folder, together with the original five source files in a private `references` folder. Do not place source documents in the website's public assets folder unless their publication is approved.
5. Paste Prompt 0 from `05-Cursor-Prompts.md` first. Then run one numbered prompt at a time, inspecting the result before continuing. Individual prompts are also supplied in `prompts/`.

`03-Content-Blueprint.md` gives the page content structure and proposed Arabic copy. `04-DESIGN.md` translates the Notion reference into an Amescom design direction. `AMESCOM-Planning-Pack.html` is a readable, printable version of the pack; it runs locally without external services.

## Working recommendation

Start with an Arabic public marketing website, structured to support English once translations are approved. Explain the four product families, connect them to the four sectors in the supplied copy, and make “Contact us” the primary journey across the site. Include support and careers as separate journeys. Language, lead handling, support scope and the availability of FRONA must be confirmed before release.

The public brand decision is resolved: Amescom is the only company name to appear on the website. The most consequential remaining issues are which claims are approved for publication and where submitted enquiries and attachments will be handled.

All proposed requirements and technical choices are recommendations until accepted. Documented product descriptions are evidence of what the supplied materials say, not independent verification of current capabilities or performance.
